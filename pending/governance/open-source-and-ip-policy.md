# Open Source & Intellectual Property (IP) Policy

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Version** | v1.0.0 |
| **Last updated** | 2026-08-25 |

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy ensures that all work funded or produced by the DAO remains a public good for the Radix ecosystem. It prevents the privatization of DAO-funded outputs by individual contributors or external entities, and it preserves the community's ability to reuse, fork, and build on the work it has paid for. The policy applies to outputs the DAO produces or funds, and — through §10 — to intellectual property the DAO receives from Asset Transferors.

---

## 2. Guiding Principle

> Work funded by the DAO must remain accessible, reusable, and beneficial to the ecosystem.

---

## 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

* software and code
* documentation and written content
* marketing and creative assets
* any deliverables funded through DAO treasury

---

## 4. Default Licensing

### 4.1 Software & Code

All software developed by Working Groups or funded through DAO proposals (including RFPs and direct grants under the Contributor Compensation Policy) must be released under a permissive open-source license. The default permitted licenses are:

* MIT License
* Apache License 2.0

A different license may be used only where it is at least as permissive as MIT or Apache 2.0 for ecosystem participants, and only with explicit approval as described in §6.

### 4.2 Content & Creative Assets

All non-code deliverables — documentation, marketing materials, graphics, and media — must be released under **Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY 4.0)**. Attribution must be preserved by the DAO in line with the license terms.

---

## 5. Ownership & Rights

The DAO does not grant exclusive ownership of funded outputs to any contributor or entity. Contributors may be credited as authors and may use the work themselves on the same terms as anyone else in the ecosystem, but they may not restrict downstream usage by other ecosystem participants. All funded outputs must remain freely usable by the ecosystem under the applicable open-source or Creative Commons license.

---

## 6. Exceptions

Any deviation from the default licensing in §4 — including the use of a license not listed in §4.1, dual licensing, or any restriction on commercial reuse — must be explicitly justified and approved via Governance Proposal before the work is commissioned. The proposal must state the specific deviation requested, the reasons, and the impact on downstream ecosystem use. Exceptions are not granted retroactively.

---

## 7. Contributor Agreements

Where the work warrants it (for example, contributions to a protocol repository or work on materials with attribution implications), contributors may be required to agree to the applicable licensing terms in writing before work begins. Deliverables that do not comply with this policy may not be accepted by the commissioning Working Group, and Steward sign-off under the Contributor Compensation Policy §7 requires confirmation that licensing terms are met.

---

## 8. Enforcement

Non-compliant deliverables may be rejected by the Working Group Steward and payment may be withheld until the deliverable is brought into compliance. Where a contributor has been paid and subsequently fails to release the work under the required license, the matter may be escalated through the **Dispute Resolution & Arbitration Policy** and, in cases of bad faith, may result in disciplinary action under the Code of Conduct.

---

## 9. Transparency

All funded work must clearly state its license in the repository or asset itself, be publicly accessible at a stable location, and be linked from the originating Governance Proposal so that the funding decision and the resulting output can be traced together.

---

## 10. Transferred Assets

This section applies to software, documentation, license rights, and other intellectual property received by the DAO from Asset Transferors, as distinct from work funded or produced by the DAO itself.

**Required transfer documentation.** Each incoming transfer must be documented by its own **Asset Transfer Agreement**, incorporating an **IP Schedule** as Exhibit A that identifies the assets transferred and the license governing each. These are per-transfer instruments: they are negotiated and executed with the Asset Transferor when that transfer is agreed, and cannot exist in advance of it. The DAO does not maintain a standing Asset Transfer Agreement or IP Schedule, and none is ratified as part of the governance framework.

The DAO may not accept a transfer of assets under this section until the Asset Transfer Agreement and its IP Schedule have been executed. Each executed Asset Transfer Agreement and IP Schedule is recorded in the governance records in accordance with **Operating Agreement §9.14**, subject to any confidentiality or redaction requirement applicable to the transfer documentation. Where an Asset Transferor requires its own form of transfer documentation, that form satisfies this section provided it identifies the transferred assets and the license governing each.

### 10.1 Applicable License

The license governing each transferred asset is the license specified in the IP Schedule (Exhibit A of the relevant Asset Transfer Agreement). The DAO does not alter the license of transferred assets without a Governance Proposal.

### 10.2 Enforcement Rights

Where a transferred asset is distributed under an open-source license that includes commercial use restrictions, the DAO holds the right to enforce those restrictions against third parties to the extent permitted by the applicable license and the terms of the Asset Transfer Agreement. Enforcement decisions require a Governance Proposal.

### 10.3 Modification and Redistribution

The DAO may modify and redistribute transferred Source Code in accordance with the applicable license. Any modification that constitutes a protocol-level change must follow the code review process in the **Source Code Stewardship Policy §5.2** and, where required, receive Governance Proposal approval before deployment.

### 10.4 No Re-Licensing Without Governance Approval

The DAO may not re-license transferred assets under a different license without an explicit Governance Proposal that identifies the asset, the proposed new license, and the rationale. Any re-licensing that restricts ecosystem access relative to the original license requires a Constitutional Amendment.

### 10.5 Attribution

The DAO shall maintain accurate attribution records for all transferred assets, crediting the original authors and preserving existing attribution notices in accordance with the applicable license.

### 10.6 Relationship to Source Code Stewardship Policy

The **Source Code Stewardship Policy** governs the operational management, maintenance, and security of transferred Source Code. This section governs the licensing and IP rights framework. Both policies apply simultaneously to transferred software assets.
